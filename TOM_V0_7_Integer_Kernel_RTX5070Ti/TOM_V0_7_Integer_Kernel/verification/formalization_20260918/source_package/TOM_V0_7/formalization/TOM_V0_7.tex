\newsec{The final Arch formula: what it actually proves}{External scalar diagnostic / the intrinsic core is not replaced}
This section reads the source's absolute values as ordinary values of finite real scalars. It is \textbf{an explicitly named diagnostic}, not a reintroduction of scalar magnitude as TOM's ontology. The formula is
\[
 \ar(a,b)=|a|+|b|-|a-b|.
\]
Direct algebra gives
\[
 \boxed{\ar(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&ab\le0,\\
 2\min(|a|,|b|),&ab>0.
 \end{cases}}
\]
\textbf{Proof.} Opposite signs give $|a-b|=|a|+|b|$. Equal nonzero signs give $|a-b|=\bigl||a|-|b|\bigr|$, leaving twice the smaller magnitude. A zero operand also gives zero. Therefore
\[
 \ar(a,b)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ab\le0.
\]

\subsection{The inverse-log equation is an identity}
Under the source's numerical choice
\[
 \varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2},\qquad k=\ln(1/\varphi)=-\ln\varphi<0,
\]
the final relation becomes
\[
 \boxed{\ar(c,kc)=0\quad\text{for every finite real }c.}
\]
The same is true for the source's jitter substitution, and for its causality/entropy substitution. \src{pp. 33--36}

\subsection{What cannot be inferred from this identity}
It does not select a particular $c$, jitter law, frequency, causal direction or entropy law. It also does not select the golden ratio uniquely: the inverse logarithm of \emph{any} positive ratio greater than one is negative and gives the same identity.

\begin{boxx}
The zero set of this reading is not merely $a=b$ or a unique crossing. It includes a whole opposite-sign region. The equation is compatible with many different defining situations, which is why V0.7 keeps the defining structure instead of reducing TOM to that zero.
