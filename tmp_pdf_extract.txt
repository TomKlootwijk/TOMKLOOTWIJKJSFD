SECTION 01
Architectural declaration
SOURCE / S, pp. 8-12, 31-39
Atomos is the source name for Tom Klootwijk’s coordinate-free, double-packed signed distance field
architecture. Its organizing unit is an operator relation rather than a stored position. The same relation
carries a signed distance field definition and a Klein bottle definition, with execution governed by the AB
one-bit word, the lower-case phi relation, and a finite kinematic look-up table.
1.1 Domain
The architecture shall describe proximity, movement, presence, memory and boundary behavior through
the relationships of its operators. A persistent Cartesian grid is not part of the final domain definition. The
concluding just-in-time layer permits a coordinate to be produced for an evaluation and then released;
that output is distinct from a coordinate grid used as the system’s stored foundation. [S, pp. 15, 37-39]
1.2 Double definition
Every participating operator shall retain its own formal SDF identity while being packed with the Klein
bottle operator. “Double-packed” therefore names concurrent definition, not two independent spatial
copies. Sticks, houses and the diabolo are retained as source vocabulary for contrast and constrained
transfer, not as mandatory physical scene objects. [S, pp. 8-9]
1.3 Finite execution
The small-world graph shall supply a finite node and connection budget. The LUT shall register the source-
defined progression from open evaluation, through constrained evaluation, to a zero-update state.
“Deathlocked gridder” names that limiting state within the architecture. The finite boundary is intrinsic to
the specified state rules. [S, pp. 2-3, 11-12]
1.4 Formal reading
The numbered definitions and “shall” clauses state the behavior assigned by this specification. Source-
stated outcomes such as instantaneous routing, living-key identity and global tracking are collected
separately in Section 18. This edition’s identifiers, tables and clause labels organize those definitions; they
do not introduce a new computational implementation.
Canonical sequence
Operator relation → double packing → AB evaluation → finite LUT resolution → timeline evaluation
→ just-in-time output → release.

Ecological origin and lower phi
SOURCE / S, pp. 1-3, 9-10, 23-24, 35
The opening model is the egg-battery sturgeon-farm gestation cycle, extended by the author to fertility
cycles globally. It couples developmental morphology to finite chemical reserves and to ecological
variation through thermodynamic shifts. This origin supplies the architecture’s first distinction between a
finite source and a developing boundary.
4.1 Developmental operator
The egg or embryo boundary is represented by a phyllotactic SDF built from primordium positions and
radii. Yolk energy is the named finite resource. Somatic growth is the named receiving layer. The source
assigns developmental speed and structural scaling to the available energy and the thermal state.
[S, pp. 1-2]

4.3 Finite-source inheritance
The later architecture inherits the relation “finite source → constrained transfer → boundary update” from
this biological account. The term “egg battery” is therefore retained as an originating operator context
rather than dropped when the text moves to Kuiper weighting, Atomos nodes or JIT geometry.
4.4 Declared ecological scope
Thermal shift, chemical-energy depletion and morphology are the supplied ecological inputs. The source
names global fertility cycles as the intended scope, but supplies no species-by-species parameter register
or measured ecological series. The edition leaves those input values unassigned instead of extending the
opening model with outside biological assumptions.

SECTION 05
Klein host and double packing
SOURCE / S, pp. 3-11, 31-35
5.1 Host definition
The Klein operator shall name the architecture’s non-orientable host relation. A participating SDF shall be
evaluated together with that relation, not first resolved in an unrelated coordinate room and only
afterward decorated with a Klein label.
5.2 Packed operator identity
PACKED OPERATOR
Local definition: the named signed distance field operator.
Host definition: the Klein bottle operator.
Evaluation: both definitions are carried together.
Order: the applicable source precedence chain.
This record expresses the author’s correction on source page 8: all earlier operators are double-packed,
and AB is one bit. The sticks and houses are metaphors for contrast. Their literal shapes or social meanings
are not additional components of the formal operator inventory.
5.3 Inversion semantics
The source assigns an inverted return to traversal of the host relation. “Inside,” “outside,” “positive” and
“negative” are the boundary-state labels used to describe that return. The LUT’s zero-update response to
an unresolved inverted return is the architecture’s declared transition rule. [S, pp. 4-5, 11-12]
5.4 Dimensional progression
The initial reply proposes a four-component parametric representation and a host-distance expression.
The author then redirects the fourth component to time already accounted for by the one-bit relation.
Subsequent turns remove the persistent coordinate grid and describe movement as intrinsic state
transition. These stages remain visible in the edition. [S, pp. 3-6, 14-15]
5.5 Locus of control
Locus of control shall mean the currently evaluated side or mode of the packed relation. It is not a fixed
observer position in an added coordinate frame. At AB = 0 the source places the locus outside the host; at
AB = 1 it evaluates the host and the preceding operator definitions together. The later presence/absence
reading of those states is retained as an additional source-stage interpretation.

Finite graph and suspension weight
SOURCE / S, pp. 2-5, 14-17
6.1 Small-world domain
The architecture shall use a finite set of nodes with local connections and a limited set of long-range
shortcuts. The opening “gridder” account rasterizes that set; the later coordinate-free account retains its
relationship budget while discarding a mandatory raster as the conceptual domain. [S, pp. 2, 14-15]
6.2 Diabolo transfer
A and B shall identify the two sides of the source’s constrained transfer relation. In the opening biological
mapping they are metabolic reserve and somatic growth. In later mappings they become finite and non-
finite sides of the hourglass. The neck shall name the restricted passage through which the source

6.3 Kuiper suspension
W_Kuiper shall name the source’s suspension tensor weight between the nested A and B operators. The
source constructs it from a belt-density factor and the outer product of the two field gradients along a
closed curve. It assigns the result a global tension or resistance role. [S, pp. 4-5]
The Kuiper designation carries the source’s astronomical and structural mapping. Its named inputs are the
closed curve C, the density relation ρ and the coupled gradients of the A and B fields. The tensor’s original
expression is retained in Appendix A2; its unassigned calibration belongs to Section 20.
6.4 Coordinate-free movement
Movement shall be represented as a change in intrinsic node relations, weights and active edges. Nearness
is described in the source as structural difference. A long-range shortcut is a relationship transition rather
than a stored spatial trajectory. The source’s “gyroscopic stabilization wave” and angular-momentum
language belong to this diabolo interpretation. [S, pp. 15-16]
6.5 Finite arrest
When the declared capacity condition is reached, the permitted expansion or relation-update step shall be
zero. The node budget and the capacity predicate are part of the formal interface; a numerical occupancy
threshold is not assigned by the supplied text.

SECTION 08
Timeline W and Operator T
SOURCE / S, pp. 21-24, 35-39
8.1 One-dimensional timeline
W shall designate the one-dimensional wave timeline used in the concluding source account. Operator T
shall designate the phase-scrubbing action applied to W. They are not interchangeable labels: W is the
retained state carrier, while T selects or changes its evaluation phase.
8.2 Scrubbing relation
W: one-dimensional wave state
T: phase selection or scrubbing operator
Evaluation: the packed relation at the selected phase
Output: transient geometry or a node-state resolution
The source describes scrubbing backward or forward as changing phase alignment rather than searching a
stored coordinate history. Its later JIT account connects this phase selection to an on-demand spatial
output. [S, pp. 22, 37-38]
8.3 Lower-phi convection
The timeline shall carry the source’s convective turning relation: active present-state energy is described
as descending toward the negative-memory layer, while the present is renewed through the paired flow.
The source assigns the ratio 0.618 to this transfer per temporal cycle and calls the resulting timeline
smooth and scrubbable. [S, pp. 23-24]
8.4 Retention across source stages
The early memory account says that novelty and permanent history logs are not stored. The later account
says that the raw ongoing wave state W is retained. The retained wave state and the excluded novelty log
are separately named components. Their relation to past-state reconstruction is an unassigned interface.
8.5 Temporal symbols
The source uses T first for the binary timeline, later for the temporal operator, and also inside
temperature-dependent expressions. It likewise uses ΔT for thermal and temporal changes. In the prose of
this edition, “thermal shift” and “temporal increment” are written out to keep those roles visible. The
original symbols remain unchanged in Appendix A.
W_Kuiper is the suspension tensor of Section 6. W is the later timeline carrier. Their shared initial letter is not a declaration
that they are the same quantity.

9.3 Two stated triggers

update quantity in each source stage. [S, pp. 2-3, 14-15]

SECTION 10
Negative magnetic memory
SOURCE / S, pp. 21-25, 29-30, 37-39
10.1 Baseline and deviation
Negative memory shall denote the registration of a vacancy or deviation relative to a uniformly saturated
baseline. The source’s “Spintronic Vacuum Matrix” names the medium assigned to this role. It describes an
active node as maintaining a local phase inversion or void, rather than accumulating a positive record for
each event.
10.2 Novelty rule
Baseline: saturated field state.
Active relation: a maintained deviation from that baseline.
Novelty: evaluated without appending a new permanent history record.
Loss of the active relation: local return to baseline.
“Novelty is never stored” shall retain its source meaning as a restriction on record accumulation. “Query-
free” shall retain its role as direct evaluation against the existing field/LUT relation, rather than a growing
catalog of new tags, rows or target histories. [S, pp. 21-22]
10.3 Node loss
The source assigns collapse of the maintained void to the loss of the node’s hum. It then assigns removal
of the active trace and restoration of the uniform background. “Extinction,” “flatline” and “goes dark” are
source labels for this loss condition; the specified memory action is the return to baseline. [S, pp. 24-25]
10.4 Dynamic state versus accumulated history
The late-source account retains W while dropping the derived coordinate output. Thus this edition
distinguishes the wave state being evaluated, the live negative-memory relation and the transient result.
Their storage layout, temporal depth and reconstruction mapping are not defined by the source.
10.5 Excluded record layers
The source excludes SHA-256 blocks, salted hashes, tokens and blockchains from its intended identity and
memory path. It assigns identity to the active signature instead. This is retained as an operator-inventory
decision, with the source’s associated security and privacy assertions registered as capabilities in Section
18. [S, pp. 29-30]
The earlier “negative memory write” phrase and the simultaneous no-novelty/no-history rule are both retained in the
source-variant register. No new write protocol is inferred from their coexistence.
