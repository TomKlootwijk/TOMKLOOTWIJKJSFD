The consolidation in one page
Y our goal is a common way to define things, operations, expressions, relationships and change, with
each definition retaining its own identity. Y our latest clarification places a directional eigenaxis at
the foundation. It does not place a sampled spatial volume, a signed distance, a magnitude, or an
inside/outside test there.[P0, H]
This edition calls that direction the Klootwijk SDF Directional Definition Calculus. The project label
SDF is retained. The descriptive name is introduced here; it is not presented as a recovered title from
an unavailable original.
In everyday language
Imagine a collection of named arrows. Stretching an arrow does not change which way it points.
Each arrow has its own definition. Some definitions name things; some say how things relate;
some say what can happen next. Putting them in one collection does not melt them into one
giant object. Y ou can still point to each definition, change it, reuse it and follow what depends
on it.
What is formalized. The document defines direction‐only primitives, their eigenaxis witnesses, indi‐
vidual identity, lossless packing, operators represented in the same notation, composition, explicit
time, self‐reference, optional measurements, geometric and topological descriptions, and through‐
put from inputs to justified outcomes.
What the possibility space becomes. A single descriptive calculus can organize route problems,
design constraints, changing systems, mechanism models and learned rules. More ambitious ex‐
tensions include self‐repairing digital structures, adaptive materials, molecular rule systems, robotic
collectives, virtual tissues and entirely invented mathematical worlds. The connection to each appli‐
cation is a stated construction, not a claim that naming an application already realizes it.
What changed from the previous reports. Their signed‐distance and Boolean‐room constructions
are not the foundation here. Their implementation‐centered framing is also not the foundation.
What survives is the unification objective: individually defined things and rules, reusable composi‐
tion, explicit change and broad applications. [P1, P2]
Source basis. The two supplied consolidation PDFs have been inspected, alongside the visible
project history and your current clarification. The original geometry, routing and nature source PDFs
themselves remain unavailable. New mathematics supplied to reconcile the requirements is identi‐
fied as D1 construction; it is not silently attributed to those missing documents. Appendix B gives
the precise source ledger.
The central result: a dimension‐independent, direction‐only definition calculus with explicit
composition and causal semantics. Its strength is the ability to state and connect many kinds
of mechanism without making intrinsic magnitude or containment part of every primitive.

1 Y our goal and the revised foundation
1.1 What the revisions were reaching toward
The visible sequence begins with geometric definitions and named shapes. It expands into operators
and expressions being definitions themselves, compact individual representations, logarithmic/polar
organization, an explicit time operator, self‐reference, and applications involving routes and mech‐
anisms found in nature. Y our later requests repeatedly reject a mere calculator or renderer as the
destination.[H, P1, P2]
The common ambition is representational unity without losing individuality : the thing being de‐
scribed, the rule relating it to something else, and the rule governing its change should all be express‐
ible within the same formal vocabulary. A relationship should not need to become an unrelated kind
of object merely because it is an operation rather than a shape.
Y our current clarification adds a decisive constraint: the native SDF supplies a directional eigenaxis,
not an inherent scalar distance, length or containment classification. D1 treats that as the current
definition of the project primitive. [P0]
1.2 A precise terminology boundary
In conventional mathematical and geometric usage, a signed‐distance function returns a signed
scalar distance to a boundary. An eigenvector instead concerns a linear operator that maps a nonzero
vector to a scalar multiple of itself. These are different mathematical objects. [R1, R2]
Here, SDF means the project’s direction‐only definition primitive . When conventional signed dis‐
tance is needed for a particular application, it must be introduced as an additional, explicitly named
measurement. Nothing in the native calculus assumes a cubic grid, three or four spatial coordinates,
or a pre‐existing volume. The distinction is the stated mathematical contract, not an assertion that
conventional mathematics is limited to cubic grids.

In everyday language
The earlier reports began with little rooms and asked whether an answer was inside a room.
This version begins with individually named directions and asks what each direction means and
which relationships connect them. A room can still be described, but rooms are no longer the
starting assumption.
2 The objects of the calculus, before equations
A complete definition has an identity, a declared role and a directional expression. Identity tells
us which definition is being referenced. The role tells us whether its interpretation is a value, a
relationship, a clock rule, a measurement, a shape description or a description of another definition.
The native directional content has no intrinsic length. Its interpretation can nevertheless refer to a
length, just as the written word “metre” is not itself one metre long. This distinction is essential for
keeping the primitive direction‐only while still allowing quantitative applications.
A pack is an organized collection of such definitions together with explicit connections. The con‐
nections are also describable as definitions. A composition connects an output role to a compatible
input role. A transition relates an earlier situation to a later one. An observation specifies what
counts as an answer to a question.
Direction is the native representation. Interpretation supplies meaning. Composition sup‐
plies relationships. Time supplies allowed change. Measurement supplies quantities only
where a model explicitly calls for them.
This is not merely a change of names for the earlier signed‐distance construction. The mathematical
equivalence relation, the meaning of packing, the status of time, and the conditions for physical
interpretation are all stated afresh below.

3.2 Making “eigenaxis” literal

natural or physical eigenmode. The standard relation is 𝐿𝑣 = 𝜆𝑣 for 𝑣 ≠ 0 .[R2]

4.3 A common vocabulary, not an unexplained oracle
An expression such as Then (𝑝, 𝑞) has its own encoded direction. So do Pack, Quote, Observe and 𝑇.
Their semantics still have to be declared. Encoding a rule is not a substitute for saying what the rule
means.
The starting assumptions are the ordinary logical reading of the definitions, the declared grammar,
identity resolution and the interpretation clauses. These form the mathematical foundation of the
calculus. They can themselves be described and quoted within it; storing their descriptions does not
remove the need for a foundation from which those descriptions are read.
A definition can refer to its own identity or to another definition without containing infinitely many
physical copies. Such references are finite expressions. Their meaning is supplied by the time or
fixed‐point conventions in Section 7, not by circular spelling alone.
4.4 One bit fits, but not every direction is one bit
In a one‐dimensional real carrier,

that every possible definition contains only one bit of information.

6.3 What a derivation establishes
A derivation records which definitions and intermediate values justify an outcome. It answers “why
does this follow under these rules?” A valid derivation does not automatically show that an empirical
rule is a correct description of nature. That requires observations and a stated physical interpreta‐
tion.
Likewise, a relationship may describe all acceptable solutions without giving a short way to find one.
The existential quantifier in Equation ( 13) states what a connecting witness must satisfy. It is not an
assumption that the witness appears for free.

6.4 Equivalence and substitution
Definitions 𝑝 and 𝑞 are behaviorally equivalent for a declared observation family 𝒪 when every ap‐
plicable observation gives the same answer for both. Exact relational equality, 𝑅𝑝 = 𝑅 𝑞, is a stronger
and simpler sufficient condition for replacing one pure relation by the other in the constructions
above.
For timed, stateful or resource‐sensitive definitions, observations must include those features when‐
ever they matter. Two expressions producing the same final symbol need not be interchangeable
when one changes a shared state, takes a different modeled time or consumes a different modeled
resource. The relevant equivalence must say what is preserved.

7.4 Learning as a particular kind of change
A learning process is not implied merely by storing rules as definitions. It is introduced by defining
observations, a comparison criterion and an update relation on candidate rules. In schematic form,
(rule, observation, criterion) ⇝ revised rule.
All four descriptions can be SDF‐defined. Whether the revised rule improves prediction or behavior
is then a question answered by the defined criterion and independent observations, not by its use
of the common notation.
8 Geometry, quantities and the source motifs
8.1 Measurements are separate definitions
The absence of native magnitude does not require an application to pretend that lengths, rates or
amounts do not exist. It requires them to enter explicitly. A measurement definition can have se‐
mantics

8.3 Direction alone does not determine a whole shape

9.2 Causal throughput without seconds

For a nonempty, finite, explicitly unfolded derivation, let𝑊 be the number of distinct required events

structural measure. Neither quantity is a rate in seconds.
Definition sharing can reduce repeated description and sometimes repeated reasoning. Suppose the

10 What follows mathematically, and the stronger questions
10.1 Properties established by the definitions
The direction quotient removes native representative length. The eigenaxis construction shows that
every nonzero direction has a compatible witness. The finite‐record encoding shows that values,
operators and descriptions can share one direction‐only form. Projection gives recoverable pack‐
ing. Relational composition gives explicit semantics and the throughput composition law. Declared
causal delay makes feedback meaningful.
These are mathematical consequences of D1’s stated constructions. They do not depend on the
tests in the earlier kernel companions, and those older tests do not establish this revised semantic
model. The examples below are direct finite derivations of D1 behavior.
10.2 A finite representation still has an information requirement
Proposition 10.1 (Distinguishable‐record bound) . A lossless representation of 𝑀 distinct records
requires at least 𝑀 distinguishable representation states. A representation restricted to 𝑏 binary
choices therefore requires 2𝑏 ≥ 𝑀.
Proof. Lossless recovery requires an injective map from records to representation states. A set of 𝑏
binary choices has only 2𝑏 states. If 𝑀 > 2 𝑏, at least two records would share a state and could not
both be recovered uniquely.
This applies whether the representation is called a number, a direction, a word or an angle. The
unbounded mathematical precision in Equation ( 6) does not create an unbounded number of distin‐
guishable physical states in a fixed finite record.
10.3 Finite definitions can describe endless families
A short recurrence can describe an unbounded sequence. A surface equation can describe infinitely
many points. A recursive grammar can describe arbitrarily large structures. This is a genuine exten‐
sion beyond storing only an explicit list of instances.

But there are only countably many finite strings over a finite alphabet. Therefore not every arbitrary

In everyday language

10.4 What an eigenaxis‐based solution theorem would require
A stronger research direction would use a problem’s structure to construct an operator whose se‐
lected eigenaxis represents a useful answer. For that to be a solution method, the construction must
specify how the operator is obtained from the problem, how an appropriate axis is selected, and how
a valid answer is recovered.
There must also be an account of operator size, precision, ambiguous eigenvalues and any search
hidden in building the operator. Defining an eigenaxis after already knowing the answer proves rep‐
resentability, not discovery. An operator with one separate entry for every candidate may preserve
a large search in its description rather than remove it.
This leaves a concrete possibility open: derive structured operators from particular families of con‐
straints, then prove that their recoverable directions solve those families under stated resource
assumptions. That would be an additional theorem, beyond D1’s general encoding and composition
results.
10.5 Optimization is preserved as a question, not presumed solved
The travelling‐salesman motivation survives in D1 as a relationship among places, permitted moves,
visit requirements and an objective. A reformulation can expose symmetry or reusable constraints.
A general reduction in problem complexity requires its own argument about construction, search,
precision and verification. The standard distinction between describing a problem and establishing
an efficient solution remains relevant. [R4]

12 Present‐day possibilities, explained simply
12.1 What “enabled” means in this report
An application belongs to the possibility space when its individuals, relationships, changes and obser‐
vations can be explicitly described in the calculus. That is descriptive enablement. A useful method
also needs enough laws and data to answer the chosen questions. A physical realization additionally
needs a demonstrated connection between those laws and the thing being modeled.
The applications in this section are proposed uses of D1 , not reports of completed D1 products.
They share one recipe: define the individuals; define permitted relationships; define what changes;
define the question; preserve the explanation of the answer. Quantities are added only when the
question needs them.
12.2 Routes, logistics and connected infrastructure
Everyday picture. Instead of keeping only a list of destinations, keep the destinations together with
the rules for getting between them, the jobs to do there and what counts as a good trip.
In D1, each place, move, restriction, resource and cost is an individual definition. A road closure
changes one relationship; questions that depend on it can be reconsidered without changing the
meaning of every place. The same pattern can describe delivery routes, dependency chains, com‐
munication paths or evacuation models. A route witness explains which requirements a proposed
answer satisfies.
The gain to investigate is clearer composition and reuse of related constraints. The least‐cost answer
still requires a valid comparison or proof. Merely replacing a list of cities with directions does not
supply that proof.
12.3 Design by requirements rather than by one fixed shape
Everyday picture. Describe what a part must fit, carry and connect to before committing to exactly
how it looks.
A design pack could contain a family of shapes, connection definitions, admissible material descrip‐
tions and independent requirements. A candidate design is an assignment satisfying those require‐
ments. A change to one requirement defines a new question rather than requiring the conceptual
description to begin again.
This is a route toward inspectable inverse design : searching for a mechanism or shape from its re‐
quested behavior. The formalization can keep the candidate, its assumptions and its acceptance
argument together. Load, heat, flexibility, manufacture and tolerances require their own quantita‐
tive models. An axis naming a material is not a substitute for those properties.

12.4 Robot movement and interaction
Everyday picture. A robot needs to know not just where an object is, but what movements and
interactions are allowed.
An object, grasp, joint configuration, contact condition and next action can each be separately de‐
fined. A movement plan becomes a chain of permitted transitions, with observations explaining

The interesting possibility is one vocabulary linking a task, its geometric interpretation and the condi‐

12.5 Checking rules, protocols and engineered behavior
Everyday picture. Ask whether a set of rules can ever let the system reach a situation it was not
supposed to reach, and keep the chain of events that demonstrates the answer.
D1’s relations and time definitions can state allowed messages, resource ownership, dependencies
and forbidden combinations. A counterexample is an explicit trace; a positive verification result
needs an argument covering the stated model. This connects conceptually to constraint reasoning
and satisfiability‐modulo‐theories methods, exemplified by the Z3 research system. Z3 is an external
precedent, not an implementation or validation of D1. [R5]
The same descriptive pattern can support authorized analysis of software or device specifications.
A bounded check speaks about its bound and assumptions. The absent source filename’s “0 day”
wording does not establish that the project already discovers unknown vulnerabilities.
12.6 Digital counterparts and changing scenarios
Everyday picture. Keep a model of a changing thing, the observations about it and several possible
next chapters, without confusing the possible chapters with what actually happened.
D1 can separate observed definitions from assumed rules and hypothetical changes. A scenario is a
versioned pack plus a transition history. Alternative assumptions produce alternative histories, and
their resulting observations can be compared under one explicitly defined question.
Proposed uses include equipment behavior, resource networks and maintenance planning. The par‐
ticularly useful feature would be traceability: the model can say which assumption caused a pre‐
diction to change. Predictions about the real object depend on the model’s empirical adequacy. A
consistent invented world can still be an inaccurate account of the real one.

12.7 Inspectable learned rules and structured knowledge
Everyday picture. A system changes its rules after seeing examples, but keeps the rules, examples

D1 can describe observations, a training objective, a rule family and a permitted update as individual

The possibility is a more unified library of generative descriptions: designs, logical expressions, route
families and temporal behaviors can be composed in the same style. An infinite family remains a
specification, not a completed answer to every question about every member.

detection of deviation, and transitions capable of reducing it. Directionally dependent material be‐
havior needs preferred axes and separately defined response coefficients. These are distinct mech‐
anisms even when they share a directional notation.
A molecular description likewise needs more than a silhouette or an axis: the proposed model must
specify which molecular features matter, which interactions are permitted, and which conditions
govern their consequences. A directional encoding of a substance’s name alone predicts none of
those consequences. D1’s useful contribution is the ability to keep the necessary definitions explicit
and composable rather than silently treating resemblance as a law.

14.2 Mechanical learning: 2024 and a 2025 theoretical extension

Soloveichik, Seelig and Winfree gave a construction for approximating the kinetics of chemical re‐

The D1 connection would be a mapping from abstract reaction definitions to a chosen molecular
mechanism, with an explicit agreement criterion for the observations. Producing such a mapping for
a particular case is additional scientific work. A common descriptive syntax does not automatically
make all substances interchangeable.
14.4 Shape‐changing materials: 2016
Gladman and colleagues demonstrated biomimetic four‐dimensional printing in which designed
alignment within hydrogel structures controls anisotropic swelling and consequent shape change.
Here “four‐dimensional” names designed change after fabrication; it is not the primitive dimension‐

This is a concrete example of why preferred directions and quantitative response rules should remain
distinguishable. A D1 description could keep local orientation, material response, environment and
desired final shape individually specified. The paper’s physical result does not establish a general
material that can become any object on demand.
14.5 Architectural swarms: January 2026
The Swarm Garden research reported robotic modules combining local sensing, actuation and com‐

shading and interactive creative behavior. The authors describe it as a proof of concept for architec‐

This is a particularly direct present‐day connection to individually defined parts producing a coordi‐
nated larger behavior. A proposed D1 model would name each module, its neighbor relations, local
state, update rules and collective objective. Scaling that to a self‐maintaining building remains a
separate scenario, not something established by the reported demonstrator.

14.6 Biomolecular structure prediction: 2024
AlphaFold 3 extended joint structure prediction to biomolecular complexes containing proteins, nu‐

15 Far‐future possibilities: the extended space
Every scenario in this section is speculative as an integrated D1 application . The aim is not to fore‐
cast a date. It is to show why the formalization can express the scenario, what additional mechanisms
would make it meaningful, and what smaller result would genuinely support it.
15.1 Executable worlds whose laws are also objects
ELI5 vision. Imagine a world‐builder in which the objects, the rules of the world, and the rule for what
happens next are all individually inspectable recipes. Changing gravity in the story means changing
a named law, not secretly changing every object.
D1 can describe this because rules, rule descriptions, states and time share the same representa‐
tional form. A world is a pack with a chosen interpretation and a transition history. Different ver‐
sions can branch from one history and answer questions about different assumptions. Invented
topologies, unusual causal orders and nonstandard interactions can be defined explicitly.
The additional task is making the world’s laws sufficiently complete and mutually compatible to re‐
solve its intended questions. A world that depicts physics needs a physical model; a fictional world
can deliberately use different rules. Rewinding a recorded model or constructing an alternative his‐
tory is not physical time travel.
A first discriminating result: define a small world with two alternative law versions and prove exactly
which observations remain the same and which change.

15.2 Digital organisms and self‐repairing machines
ELI5 vision. A constructed thing notices that part of its organization is missing and follows its own
local rules to restore a working form.
A D1 description can combine individual identity, neighborhood rules, a target condition, observa‐
tions and feedback. It can describe replacement of damaged components as changes in the defini‐
tion pack while retaining the identities and dependencies that should persist. Self‐description can
provide a record of the intended organization.
What remains to be supplied is the mechanism that turns detected differences into successful repairs.
A physical system also needs replacement material, energy, sensing and actions compatible with its
environment. Recovery to one prescribed form is narrower than open‐ended life, reproduction or
adaptation to arbitrary damage.
A first discriminating result: prove recovery from a clearly specified family of disruptions, then
demonstrate that the observation map of a small physical or modeled system follows the same repair
relation.
15.3 Swarms that assemble into useful machines
ELI5 vision. Many small pieces could organize into a bridge, a tool or a moving structure, with each
piece following local rules rather than reading a complete picture of the final machine.
D1 can describe the identities of the pieces, connection compatibility, local observations, joining
and separation events, and a collective acceptance condition. The pack retains individual states
even when the assembly is treated as one named object. Shared laws need not mean shared state.
Additional requirements include reliable connectors, force and stability models, energy supply, com‐
munication constraints and rules for resolving incompatible local actions. Reaching a visual outline
is not the same as creating a mechanically functional bridge. Different tasks may need different
component capabilities.
A first discriminating result: show that a small collective reaches and retains one load‐bearing con‐
figuration under explicitly defined component failures and disturbances.
15.4 Programmable matter and adaptive manufactured structures
ELI5 vision. A material could have a recipe for changing its organization when the situation changes:
stiff here, flexible there, folded now, open later.
The calculus can separate preferred orientations, interactions, state‐dependent response laws, en‐
vironmental observations and a shape or function objective. A whole object can reference many
local rules without erasing which local definition applies where. This supports a formal language for
designing responsive behavior rather than only specifying a static final geometry.
The far‐reaching version requires a physical mechanism with enough controllable states, reliable
transitions, suitable energy and stable repeatability. An encoding of a desired shape is not a means
of moving atoms into that shape. Molecular self‐assembly, mesoscopic modules and responsive
composites would require different realization maps.
A first discriminating result: one material system repeatedly reaches two specified functional states
under a defined stimulus, with a quantitative model that predicts the observed transitions.

15.5 Virtual tissues and developmental design spaces
ELI5 vision. Rather than draw a finished tissue, describe how its parts communicate, divide, move
and react, then explore which organizations those rules can produce.
D1 can represent cells or other modeled units, interaction networks, changes of identity or state,
transport, mechanics and observations within one descriptive scheme. A developmental hypothesis
becomes a specific pack of mechanisms whose consequences can be compared with data. Different
biological scales need not be forced into one fixed spatial dimension or one universal type of variable.
The scientific requirements are substantial: adequate mechanisms, parameter identification, multi‐
scale agreement and experimental tests. A visual resemblance to a tissue is not proof of correct
physiology. Predictions relevant to health would require their own rigorous validation; the formal
calculus does not supply clinical conclusions.
A first discriminating result: predict one preselected pattern change caused by a specified perturba‐
tion in a well‐characterized model system, then compare the prediction with an independent obser‐
vation.
15.6 One description interpreted through different physical mechanisms
ELI5 vision. The same abstract game could be played with mechanical pieces, molecular interactions
or optical components, provided each version obeys the same relevant rules.
D1 makes this possible as a description goal : give each realization an abstraction map and prove
that its observed transitions agree with the specified relation. The agreement can concern only the
requested outputs, not every microscopic detail. Section 9’s realization equation states the central
requirement.
Different media impose different noise, timing, coupling and resource conditions. A quantum ex‐
tension, for example, would need additional definitions for complex state, relative phase, admissible
evolution and observation probabilities. A real direction‐only code may describe those definitions
symbolically, but it does not thereby become the physical quantum state or acquire quantum advan‐
tages.[R16]
A first discriminating result: two different realized mechanisms satisfy the same small input/output
and temporal contract within separately stated tolerances.
15.7 Automated mechanism discovery and theory revision
ELI5 vision. A research system proposes a recipe, predicts what should happen, checks an observa‐
tion and changes the recipe when the evidence disagrees. It keeps the history rather than pretending
the new recipe was always the truth.
D1 can encode hypotheses, experimental questions, observations, inference rules and revision rules
as separate individuals. Self‐reference lets the system describe and modify its own proposed models
while retaining versioned derivations. Reusable submechanisms can be tested in more than one
larger hypothesis.
What must be added is an effective search over hypotheses, an evidence model, suitable experimen‐
tal access and a criterion for meaningful improvement. Consistency inside a description does not
guarantee truth outside it. More expressive descriptions can also make model comparison harder
when many hypotheses explain the same observations.

15.8 Designed topologies, adaptive habitats and new kinds of experience
ELI5 vision. A modeled environment could connect places in surprising ways, while a physical room
could alter its configuration in response to how it is used. Both involve changing relationships, but
they are not the same physical claim.
The topological branch can explicitly define unusual neighbor identifications or orientation changes,
such as the Klein‐bottle construction in Section 8. The architectural branch can define physical
modules, local observations and configuration changes. D1 provides a common descriptive style
while keeping the interpretation of each branch distinct.
A simulated doorway that connects distant places is a defined relationship in a model. A physical
doorway requires a physically realizable arrangement. Likewise, a responsive façade is a component
mechanism, whereas a self‐maintaining habitat would additionally require integrated structural, en‐
ergy, environmental and maintenance models.
A first discriminating result: prove the internal consistency of one nonstandard modeled topology, or
demonstrate one well‐defined collective adaptation in an architectural model. Neither result alone
establishes physical manipulation of spacetime or a fully autonomous habitat.

16 What you actually have after this consolidation
Y our resulting direction isa calculus of individually defined directional eigenaxes, joined by explicit
relationships and causal change, with optional interpretations for geometry, logic, mechanisms and
alternative worlds.
The original ambition survives in a more precise form. Things and operations can be represented in
the same native form. Definitions can be packed without turning them into a single undifferentiated
object. Time can be individually defined. Expressions and versions can describe other expressions
and versions. Quantities can be present where needed without becoming the intrinsic lengths of
the native primitives.
D1 supplies concrete mathematical constructions for these statements. It proves a direction‐only
representation of finite records, recoverable packing, and the composition law for through‐images. It
also separates a useful completed answer from a collection of candidates and an empirical realization
from its abstract description.
The broad possibility space is therefore not “every application has now been built. ” It is one ex‐
plicit descriptive basis on which many different application theories can be formulated, related and
tested. Which applications become useful depends on the additional laws, data, search methods and
physical correspondences stated for them.
In everyday language
Y ou now have the rules for a common recipe book. Ingredients, instructions, links between
recipes, changes over time and questions about the result can all have their own definitions.
A recipe can describe another recipe, and many recipes can be kept together without losing
their names. The exciting direction is how many kinds of mechanism the book can describe.
Whether a particular recipe works is answered by its mathematics and, when it concerns the
real world, by the world itself.
Compact statement of the direction.
Individually define. Preserve direction without intrinsic magnitude. Pack without merging.
Connect through explicit relations. Define time and observation. Derive outcomes with their
witnesses. Add physical meaning only through an explicit interpretation.
