01 / SELECTED ROUTE
Exact operator core; demand‐driven readouts
A signed‐field operator system derives candidates from available evidence, qualifies publication by for‐
ward time, preserves committed definitions, and constructs application readouts only when requested.
A coordinate grid, metric, graph, physical carrier and hardware backend are not mandatory parts of the
definition.

ping binary gate, finite‐capacity, lookup and just‐in‐time requirements [B]. This consolidation’s explicit
reconciliation choices are marked [U]; they are not attributed retroactively to either source. [A pp. 1–6; B
pp. 8–12, 35–39]

04 / MEMORY AND LOOKUP
Preserve evidence; discard derived workspace
Resolve non‐erasure versus “novelty is not stored” [U]
The authoritative state is the committed operator history plus the source and evidence needed to re‐
produce it. Candidate scratch space, temporary coordinates and recomputable readout caches are dis‐
posable. An accepted observation or defining term is not disposable merely because its value is novel.
[A pp. 2–3, 6; B pp. 21–25, 38–39]
The retained meaning of “no novelty storage” is narrower: unfamiliar inputs do not automatically cre‐
ate new operators, laws or permanent geometry. An unmatched input may return OUT_OF_PROFILE; an
accepted symbolic input retains its provenance. This resolves the conflict explicitly rather than claiming
both total erasure and exact historical recovery.
Finite capacity is an admission rule
Declare limits for exact‐value limbs, term records, source/evidence bytes, history, indexes and transient
workspace. Reserve the complete candidate’s required capacity before publication. Exhaustion returns
CAPACITY and leaves the committed prefix unchanged. With fixed finite storage and non‐erasure, exe‐
cution can stop; it cannot promise indefinitely growing history. [A pp. 3, 6, 8; B pp. 2–3; U]
This replaces B’s physical “deadlock” claim with a precise computational rule: no admitted update be‐
yond the budget . It does not imply that topology stops physical motion or that every occupied graph
prohibits every state transition. [B pp. 5, 12, 32–33; U]
Demand‐driven readout and finite lookup [U]
For a declared profile 𝑃, form a readout only on request:
𝑅𝑃 ,𝜏 = 𝐷 𝑃 (𝑇read(𝜏 )) .
A cache or lookup table may accelerate 𝐷𝑃 only when its exact key includes all semantically relevant
inputs: role and operands, registry/profile version, algebra basis, temporal state and evidence identity.
A hit returns the same term or status as the uncached evaluator. A miss invokes that evaluator or returns
a declared rejection; it never manufactures a nearest answer. [A pp. 3, 8; B pp. 37–39; U]
A log‐polar or spherical lookup format is optional. It requires a declared domain, radial/phase interpreta‐
tion, exact bin‐assignment rule, error bounds where quantized, and capacity. Naming a table “log‐polar”
does not supply those laws or eliminate key construction, evidence ingestion, exact arithmetic or output
cost. [B pp. 2–3, 20, 26; U]
Selected optimizations [U]
Store source bytes once with immutable references; share repeated evaluations of identical full inputs;
retain defining operands even when readouts coincide; compute geometry only when a bound readout
asks for it. No full‐space rasterization, all‐pairs graph or precomputed global route table is required by
the core.
Historical replay needs retained records and evidence, or an explicitly specified generator that repro‐
duces the requested information. Discarded observations do not become recoverable by moving a time‐
line selector. Cache eviction and authoritative‐history deletion are different operations.
