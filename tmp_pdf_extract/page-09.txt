AB, Psi and the 2 Hz relation
SOURCE  /  S, pp. 5-11, 16-17, 33-35
7.1  One-bit word
AB shall denote one bit with the values 0 and 1. This follows the author’s explicit definition on source 
page 8. The four-word expression printed in the following reply is preserved as a source variant, not 
adopted as a second, unstated bit width.
State Author-defined gate meaning Later source reading
0 Locus outside the Klein operator. Ambient or baseline evaluation; absent or inactive 
node.
1 Concurrent Klein and formal SDF definition of the 
preceding operators. Active hot-node or hum-blip evaluation.
7.2  Psi bifurcation
Psi shall name the bifurcation associated with the binary transition. In the early pseudocode, its two 
branches receive complementary expressions scaled by lower-case phi and a temporal increment. “Black 
house,” “white house,” “stick A” and “stick B” record that early contrast; the author subsequently specifies 
that these are metaphors, not separate structural objects. [S, pp. 6-8]
7.3  Double sinusoid
The “double sinusoidal 2 Hz mid-Theta hum” shall retain that exact source designation. The source assigns 
it the role of a paired, out-of-phase pattern whose jitter drives the AB evaluation relation. The phrase 
names the architecture’s clock and phase vocabulary; this edition does not substitute a different frequency 
label or waveform.
7.4  Clock interpretation
The source states two complete cycles per second and elsewhere describes two boundary checks per 
second. It does not specify an edge-triggering convention, phase offset or sampling rule that connects 
those statements. The paired-wave relation and the AB word are therefore kept distinct: the wave is the 
named timing pattern, and the bit is the evaluated state.
