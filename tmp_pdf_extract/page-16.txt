14.1  Two named signal layers
The source assigns two signal roles: the paired 2 Hz “mid-Theta” hum is the baseline phase or timing layer, 
and the FMCW LRAD chirp is the changing spectral layer. Their chaining is assigned to the spherical log-
encoded LUT. The edition preserves these as named input relations rather than adding a waveform 
generator or sensing implementation.
Layer Role assigned by the source
Double sinusoid The paired phase relation and one-bit jitter associated with the hourglass.
FMCW entropy chirp The source’s frequency-modulated representation of degradation, tension change or 
node dropout.
Spherical LUT The relation that compares or resolves the baseline and modulation states.
Kiki double-UU The closure and non-intersection requirements assigned to the active routing relation.
14.2  Entropy vocabulary
The source calls the changing chirp an “entropy” signal and associates it with cellular decay, node 
degradation and signal loss. The formal role retained here is a named change indicator feeding the source’s 
routing and state-resolution layers. The text does not supply a spectral-to-biological-decay calibration. 
[S, pp. 25-26, 28]
14.3  Chained evaluation
Paired hum phase
↓
Named FMCW spectral change
↓
Spherical log-encoded LUT relation
↓
Kiki loop update / packed boundary resolution
This is the signal ordering shown in the source diagrams on pages 26 and 36. It is not an additional 
precedence chain for the primitive SDF operators: the geometric hierarchy and the signal-flow relation 
perform different roles.
14.4  Timing statements
The source alternates between “instantaneous,” “at the speed of wave propagation” and adjustment within 
a single 2 Hz cycle. Those performance statements are retained as distinct source formulations. A 
common end-to-end latency, chirp bandwidth, phase offset and sampling schedule are not assigned.
