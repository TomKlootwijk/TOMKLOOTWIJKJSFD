SECTION 03
Precedence and primitive lineage
SOURCE  /  S, pp. 1, 6-10, 32, 35
3.1  Full source precedence
The expanded hierarchy printed on source page 9 runs from lowest to highest precedence as follows. The 
arrow here means “precedes in the declared evaluation order”; it does not add an arithmetic operation.
Pyramid side → Cone → Sphere → Sturgeon gestation
→ Phyllotaxis (lower-case phi) → Klein
Each item shall remain an operator definition when packed into the higher-level host. The biological layer 
is part of this full chain, not merely a narrative example. The source’s later compact chain on page 35 
omits the gestation entry; that shorter presentation is recorded as a variant in Section 19.
3.2  Primitive roles
Operator Source-assigned role
Pyramid side A triangular side-view boundary, described as a base-to-apex constraint.
Cone A tapering or apex relation in the initial pseudocode; a later C++ listing uses a different 
expression under the same name.
Sphere A radial boundary operator, joined to the apex-centered composite in the initial geometry.
Sturgeon gestation The finite developmental and chemical-energy relation inherited from the opening biological 
model.
Phyllotaxis The primordial arrangement and lower-phi deformation relation.
Klein The non-orientable host and packed inversion relation at the top of the printed hierarchy.
3.3  Composition is stage-specific
The source first combines its apex primitives as max(pyramid, min(cone, sphere)). A later listing takes a 
maximum across pyramid, cone, sphere and phyllotaxis. These are retained as distinct source-stage 
compositions, rather than silently replaced by a single blend or intersection rule. [S, pp. 8, 10]
3.4  Final representational role
The later coordinate-free account carries the primitive names forward as intrinsic definitions. It does not 
require the final system to regenerate a pyramid mesh, cone mesh, sphere mesh or GPU raster before 
evaluating the operator relationships. [S, pp. 11, 15, 32]
